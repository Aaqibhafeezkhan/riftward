# The setting

[Design summary](README.md) · Status: the world as it stands (2026-10-27). What content should be true to: descriptions, NPCs, items, quests.

## The world in brief

**Three thousand years ago, the rifts opened.** Rising magic tore holes into somewhere else, and horrors poured through. The kingdoms of the world were near collapse when **the Agryn** raised a vast magical barrier around three of them, pushing back the dark and giving them room to rebuild: **the Middle Kingdoms**. Outside the barrier lie **the Wastes**, where the rifts' warping never stopped.

The barrier has held ever since, because the Agryn has. But he is weakening, and **the barrier is contracting**, unevenly and slowly, swallowing border towns as it draws in. He needs an apprentice to carry on, and for far longer than ever before, he hasn't found one. **Nothing is settled:** the barrier may take hundreds of years more to reach Riftwatch, or to fail; the Agryn may yet find his apprentice; and other sources of power may be found to hold it. The year is **3174 AG**.

## The Agryn

- **A line, not one man.** When his time comes, the Agryn passes his knowledge, his power and some of his personality into an apprentice, mostly subsuming them; but not wholly, and over the successions there has been **drift**. The Agryn of now is, in a sense, everyone who has carried the name, worn and reshaped by three thousand years of handing on.
- **He has endured, and is weakening.** He has gone without an apprentice far longer than ever, so he weakens, so the barrier shrinks. The whole world's danger comes down to one missing person, or to something that could take a person's place: **other sources of power** to feed the barrier (vitae on a great scale, the Wastes' cores, something the Archivist remembers) are as possible an answer as an apprentice, and each could come with a price.
- **Why he can't find one** is open: no one suitable, something hiding or killing candidates, or something in him refusing.

## The Citadel and the Archivist

- **The Citadel** is the Agryn's stronghold, somewhere in the Middle Kingdoms. Its people regulate vitae and hunt those who practise dark rune magic, relentlessly: whoever takes life force from the unwilling threatens the one being who holds the world together. It keeps officials in towns like Riftwatch, watching the vitae trade.
- **The Archivist** dwells there, and may be even older than the Agryn. Someone who has known every Agryn, and perhaps the world before the barrier, is the keeper of the deepest secrets: what the rifts are, what came before, and what the script under Riftwatch says.

## The Middle Kingdoms and the inland sea

- **Three kingdoms**, the ones the Agryn saved, surround **a large inland sea** that reaches the ocean through **a narrow channel**: think of the Mediterranean and its strait. Their heartlands and the Citadel lie south and west of Riftwatch, across the sea: somewhere for characters to travel to as they grow.
- **The barrier rings them**, so it must close or cross the channel somewhere: the strait is one of the world's most interesting places.
- **Not yet named:** the three kingdoms, their rulers, their capitals, and which one Riftwatch belongs to.

## The Wastes

- **Beyond the barrier**, the land the rifts kept: **warped and twisted flora and fauna**, magical creatures and anomalies. Its creatures carry **concentrated vitae**, which is what makes the Wastes worth the danger.
- **The barrier stops the rifts, not people.** It holds back the rifts' energies and the corruption they cause, nothing else: in the mountain passes north of Riftwatch you can simply walk across. There's no gate, no toll and no pain at the crossing itself.
- **The danger is getting lost.** The passes are easy to stray in and hard to find a way out of, and beyond them there's nowhere safe to find again. Adventurers cross to fight there and bring back vitae crystals and relics; the ones who don't come back mostly lost their way. In Riftwatch, the road to the passes runs past the Fort, which lets through only soldiers and **guild members of Silver rank** and up: the Fort guards the road, not the barrier.
- **Breaches: the powerful tear their way in.** The Wastes' creatures can't simply walk through, since they carry the corruption the barrier holds back. A powerful enough one can **tear a hole** in it, a wound that seals again before long. It brings its corruption in with it, and while the wound stays open, **lesser creatures spill through after it**. Once it seals, whatever came through is cut off on the near side, corruption and all, until someone hunts it down.
- **The black market has its own paths.** Smugglers' trails climb into the mountains away from the Fort, used by people who cross without the guild's leave to bring crystals back without the guild's cut. Talla Mirk's trade runs on them. They're rougher and less known than the pass road, so even easier to get lost on; they ask no rank, but Riftwatch's patrols walk them. **Whoever the patrols catch is taken prisoner** and locked in the dungeon beneath the keep, and getting out means a **hefty fine**, or an **obligation**: work owed to the Fort or the guild, dangerous and unpaid, until it's done.
- **In the game:** the Fort's gate onto the pass road requires `GuildRank("silver")`, checked by the tag a character wears ([What rank unlocks](quests.md#what-rank-unlocks)). The smugglers' trails are hidden exits, found by searching or by being shown (a black-market contract, a guide paid in coin or favours), so a character of any rank can reach the Wastes early, at their own risk. Patrols on the trails are mobs that arrest rather than kill: noticed (stealth against their perception, [Stealth](trees.md#stealth)), a character who yields or is beaten is taken to the **Keep Dungeon**, stripped of contraband, and held until they pay the fine or accept the obligation (a contract they must finish, from the [quest system](quests.md)). Or they break out: the dungeon's rusted iron door leads down into the sewers. The Darkwood, beyond the North Gate, stays open to everyone.
- **In the game:** the Wastes are the top of [the ladder](darkwood.md#the-ladder-from-the-sewers-to-the-deep-wood), above the deep wood; their creatures are what the [rift-touched mutations](world.md#rift-touched-mutations-planned) look like everywhere, rather than now and then. The Darkwood's Rift-Scar, a rift inside the barrier, is a first taste, and an alarming one.

## Vitae

**The life force in all living things,** in different measure.

- **Who has it:** sentient beings (people of every race) the most; plants and animals less, enough for small enchantments; the magical creatures of the Wastes it concentrated.
- **Vitae crystals:** vitae drawn out and condensed into crystal, prized for being stable and useful. **The crystal trade is Riftwatch's reason to exist** (below). There's also talk of **cores**: the black market deals in "cores and crystals".
- **What it powers:**
  - **Rune magic** (below), which spends it permanently.
  - **Enchanting:** infusing vitae into an object makes its enchantment permanent. Legal, but tightly regulated.
  - **Healing and restoration,** possible but rarely done, for the cost.
- **The cost:** vitae taken is gone for good. Willing donors may sell theirs, legally, and are often visibly the worse for it: tired, shorter-lived, easily ill. Taking it from the unwilling is among the worst crimes there are.
- **How people see it:** common folk would rather leave it alone, as a thing of greed or desperation; adventurers see a resource and an edge; the Citadel sees misuse as a threat to the world's stability and the sanctity of life. Scholars and enchanters in Riftwatch study it and argue over its limits.
- **As a game mechanic,** vitae could be a personal reserve: spent to push past limits (a roll pushed, an ability used once more than it should be) and recovered only slowly. Discussed but not decided (see [Attributes](attributes.md#open-questions)).

## Magic

- **Common magic:** studied, trained or innate, with limits on how often. In Riftward, the four casting traditions ([Magic](magic.md)).
- **Rune magic:** older, powered by vitae and spending it for good. Mostly forbidden, but the way permanent magic items are made; risky, costly, and aging to those who use it. A possible fifth tradition one day, and certainly a thing NPCs do.
- **Dark rune magic:** rune magic fuelled by the vitae of the unwilling. The height of evil, hunted by the Citadel: villains' magic.

## Riftwatch

**A border town in the north,** near the barrier with the Wastes beyond it: a short way in from the inland sea's north-eastern shore, far from the ocean, about **a thousand people**, built of stone and timber in rugged country. North of it lies wilderness, the Darkwood, and beyond that the mountains; forest lies to the south as well. Its people are pragmatic and resourceful, used to living with danger. **It's the last bastion of the northern mountain lands:** the barrier has already drawn in far enough to take the other outposts and settlements that once stood further north, and their ruins now lie in the Wastes.

- **The lost outposts:** what the barrier left behind when it drew in, now on the far side of it: forts, villages and mines, emptied or worse, warped by the corruption that came in over them. Some of Riftwatch's families came from them, and remember. For the game they're the Wastes' first destinations, ruins to explore past the passes, with relics and records to bring back, and the twisted things that live in them now.
- **Why it thrives:** it's the nearest town to the Wastes. **The Adventurers' Guild and the vitae crystal trade** are its lifeblood: adventurers cross the barrier, fight the twisted things beyond, and come back with crystals and relics, which they sell to the guild or to enchanters, dearly. A crystal-rush town at the edge of the world.
- **The river:** **river barges carry the trade down** to the sea: crystals, pelts and relics down, grain, cloth and goods up, and from the shore across the sea to the kingdoms. The barges are the town's lifeline.
- **The danger:** monsters and magical anomalies from the Wastes, and the barrier's contraction. If it keeps drawing in, Riftwatch is one of the towns it will reach some day, maybe centuries from now; it's a fear for the town's future, not this year's, but everyone from the guildmaster to the garrison captain carries it.
- **The old city beneath:** Riftwatch is built on the ruins of a much older and larger city, whose oldest parts show in the sewers ([Riftwatch](riftwatch.md#the-old-city-beneath)). Whether it's from before the barrier, and what the Archivist knows of it, is open.

## The peoples

Riftward has **ten races** ([Races](characters.md#races), decided 2026-10-27): dwarves, elves, humans, **lizardfolk**, orks, **stoneborn**, **sprites** and trolls, and **kitsune** (fox-folk tied to the fey, who can take fox form and call foxfire, growing tails as they grow in power; charm and sleep find little hold on them, so they share the elves' Fey Mind) and **halflings** (small, nimble and lucky). The **Owlin** (owl-folk: nocturnal, feathered and winged, sharp of sight and hearing, silent fliers) aren't among them for now, and aasimar and half-elves appear as NPCs.

## What it means for the game

- **Vitae crystals are the loot that matters,** the currency of the frontier: what rift-touched and Wastes creatures leave when they die (by [harvest what's cut, loot what's left](darkwood.md#harvest-whats-cut-loot-whats-left)), graded by size or purity, sold to the guild or enchanters, smuggled past the guild's cut by people like Talla Mirk. A slime's heart of the deep reads like a vitae core.
- **Guild ranks are licences to go further,** into the Wastes most of all: the Fort lets only Silver members and up onto the road to the passes, so Bone to Iron is the apprenticeship (town, sewers, forest, Darkwood) and Silver is the licence to cross.
- **Finding your way is a skill that matters.** The passes and the Wastes beyond suit zones that are easy to get lost in (exits that loop and shift, few landmarks, weather), and make survival skills (tracking, foraging, a sense of direction) and guides worth having; a way home (a known path, a marker, a recall) is worth something too.
- **Breaches are world events,** the [rats' breakout](world.md#world-events) turned around: a boss breaks *in*, and its underlings follow. A breach opens somewhere along the barrier (in the passes, mostly), a powerful creature arrives, lesser ones spawn from the wound for as long as it stays open, and corruption spreads around it (rift-touched mutations, tainted plants) while they live. The wound seals on a timer; the creatures that came through stay until they're killed. Killing the boss is the guild's urgent contract, and the Fort's.
- **The barrier is a slow clock.** The town's fears (Kaelyn's, Marin's, Captain Thorne's) are all the same fear, but it's measured in generations: Riftwatch won't fall in a character's lifetime unless the world's story says so. What players feel is the weather of it, through world events: anomalies on patrol, breaches now and then. Whether it ever closes in, or the Agryn or some new power holds it, is the world's long story, for GMs to move on purpose, never a timer running down by itself.
- **Corruption reaches inside:** the Rift-Scar in the Darkwood, Narina's corrupted plants, rift-touched beasts in the forest and the undercity: signs, perhaps, that the barrier is weakening.
- **NPCs with names:** the townsfolk can take the names and descriptions above, and new ones (the guildmaster, the captain, the barkeep, the cook, Jyrin's shop, Brynn's forge) fill the town out ([#12](https://github.com/Foxworks-Studios/riftward/issues/12)).
- **Enchanting and rune magic** are crafts and magic for later: turning crystals into lasting item conditions (`Imbue` and item conditions are most of the machinery), and a forbidden tradition for villains, maybe for players.

## Open questions
